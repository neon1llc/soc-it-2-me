title: Glossary
description: "The SOC 2 and audit words used in this course, in plain language."
---

**Bridge letter.** A company's own letter covering the time between the end of its last report period and today, saying nothing material has changed.

**Carve-out.** A way of treating a vendor in your report: the vendor is named, its controls aren't tested, and readers are pointed to the vendor's own report.

**Complementary user entity controls (CUECs).** Things a service provider expects its customers to do for the provider's controls to work.

**Control.** Something the company does, on a schedule or on every event, that reduces a risk and leaves proof.

**Criteria.** The requirements a SOC 2 report measures controls against. Security (the common criteria, CC1 through CC9) is always in scope. Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity, and Privacy are optional.

**Evidence.** The record that shows a control happened: a log, a ticket, an export, a signed review.

**Exception.** A tested sample where the control didn't work as described. It appears in the report with management's response.

**Management's assertion.** The company's written statement, included in the report, that its system description is accurate and its controls met the criteria.

**Observation window.** The period a Type II report covers, usually three to twelve months.

**Population.** The complete list of times a control should have run during the window. The auditor samples from it.

**Qualified opinion.** An audit opinion saying one or more criteria weren't met. The result to avoid.

**Readiness assessment.** A review of control design before the audit, listing gaps without issuing a report.

**Subservice organization.** A vendor that performs part of the service you're reporting on, such as your cloud provider.

**System description.** The part of the report describing the service, its boundaries, its components, and its controls.

**Trust Services Criteria (TSC).** The AICPA's criteria for SOC 2 reports.

**Type I / Type II.** A Type I report covers control design on one date. A Type II covers design and operation over a window.

**Unqualified opinion.** A clean audit opinion.
